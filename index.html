What is Martinism & the O.M.P.L.?

Martinism is a stream of Western esotericism and inner spiritual alchemy rooted in 18th-century France. It synthesizes the theurgical operations of Martinez de Pasqually, the Masonic-Templar chivalry of Jean-Baptiste Willermoz, and the inward mystical philosophy known as the Way of the Heart (Voie Cardiaque) of Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin (1743–1803), who wrote under the pen name Le Philosophe Inconnu ("The Unknown Philosopher").

This archive compiles the history, branches, and independent person-to-person (d'homme à homme) lineages of the wider Martinist current. It also serves as the official home of the Ordre Martiniste des Porteurs de Lumière (O.M.P.L.), a non-bureaucratic, merit-based Martinist filiation operated as an optional historical and symbolic bridge for practitioners within the Novus Ordo Luciferi (NOL).

The Three Pillars of the Current

Modern Martinism rests on three distinct 18th-century streams that were consolidated into a formal initiatic Lodge system by Gérard Encausse (Papus) and Augustin Chaboseau during the 1888–1891 French Esoteric Revival:

1. Martinezism (Theurgical)

Martinez de Pasqually • Élus Coëns (c. 1754–1768)

Founded as L'Ordre des Chevaliers Maçons Élus Coëns de l'Univers ("Order of Knight-Masons Elect Priests of the Universe"), this stream focused on ceremonial theurgy, angelic invocation, and cosmic reconciliation as articulated in Pasqually's Treatise on the Reintegration of Beings. It was later revived in the 20th century by Robert Ambelain.

2. Willermozism (Chivalric)

Jean-Baptiste Willermoz • C.B.C.S. / R.E.R. (1778)

A disciple of Pasqually and colleague of Saint-Martin, Willermoz encoded Martinezist doctrine into the inner degrees of the Rite Écossais Rectifié (Rectified Scottish Rite) and its chivalric apex, the Chevaliers Bienfaisants de la Cité Sainte (Beneficent Knights of the Holy City), bridging Templar chivalry with inner regeneration.

3. Saint-Martin's Way of the Heart

Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin • d'homme à homme

Skeptical of elaborate ceremonial machinery and rigid lodge bureaucracy, Saint-Martin emphasized direct inward contemplation, self-knowledge, and awakening the latent divine spark within the "Man of Desire" (l'Homme de Désir), passing his transmission quietly from person to person (d'homme à homme).
