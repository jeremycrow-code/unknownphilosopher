What is Martinism & the O.M.P.L.?

Martinism is a stream of Western esotericism and inner spiritual alchemy rooted in 18th-century France. It synthesizes the theurgical operations of Martinez de Pasqually, the Masonic-Templar chivalry of Jean-Baptiste Willermoz, and the inward mystical philosophy known as the Way of the Heart (Voie Cardiaque) of Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin (1743–1803), who wrote under the pen name Le Philosophe Inconnu ("The Unknown Philosopher").

This archive compiles the history, branches, and independent person-to-person (d'homme à homme) lineages of the wider Martinist current. It also serves as the official home of the Ordre Martiniste des Porteurs de Lumière (O.M.P.L.), a non-bureaucratic, merit-based Martinist filiation operated as an optional historical and symbolic bridge for practitioners within the Novus Ordo Luciferi (NOL).

The Three Pillars & Historical Evolution of the Current

Modern Martinism rests on three distinct 18th-century streams that were later consolidated into a formal initiatic Lodge system during the 19th-century French Esoteric Revival:

1. Martinezism (Theurgical)

Martinez de Pasqually • Élus Coëns (c. 1754–1768)

Founded as L'Ordre des Chevaliers Maçons Élus Coëns de l'Univers ("Order of Knight-Masons Elect Priests of the Universe"), this stream focused on ceremonial theurgy, angelic invocation, and cosmic reconciliation as articulated in Pasqually's Treatise on the Reintegration of Beings. It was later revived in the 20th century by Robert Ambelain.

2. Willermozism (Chivalric)

Jean-Baptiste Willermoz • C.B.C.S. / R.E.R. (1778)

A disciple of Pasqually and colleague of Saint-Martin, Willermoz encoded Martinezist doctrine into the inner degrees of the Rite Écossais Rectifié (Rectified Scottish Rite) and its chivalric apex, the Chevaliers Bienfaisants de la Cité Sainte (Beneficent Knights of the Holy City), bridging Templar chivalry with inner regeneration.

3. Saint-Martin's Way of the Heart

Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin • d'homme à homme

Skeptical of elaborate ceremonial machinery and rigid lodge bureaucracy, Saint-Martin emphasized direct inward contemplation, self-knowledge, and awakening the latent divine spark within the "Man of Desire" (l'Homme de Désir), passing his transmission quietly from person to person (d'homme à homme).

The 1888–1891 Synthesis & 20th-Century Schisms

In the late 1880s, Dr. Gérard Encausse (Papus), claiming transmission via Henri Delaage, and Augustin Chaboseau, claiming transmission via Amélie de Boisse-Mortemart, exchanged initiations to consolidate their lineages and established the Ordre Martiniste (1891). They organized Saint-Martin's informal transmission into a three-degree system (Associé, Initié, and Supérieur Inconnu), supplemented by an administrative warrant (S∴I∴I∴) empowering Lodge and Heptad Masters to initiate others.

Following Papus's death in 1916 and the disruptions of World War I and II, the movement branched into several distinct historical trajectories: Masonic-restricted obediences (such as Jean Bricaud's Ordre Martiniste de Lyon), synarchic and F.U.D.O.S.I.-allied bodies (Victor Blanchard's O.M.&S. and the Rosicrucian-affiliated Traditional Martinist Order / O.M.T.), Philippe Encausse's 1958 restoration of the Papusian Ordre Martiniste, and autonomous European and North American lineages preserving religious freedom outside sectarian restrictions.

The Initiateur Libre (Free Initiator) & Sovereign Transmission

Before Papus drafted administrative statutes in 1891, Martinism had no lodges, officers, dues, or central headquarters. Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin transmitted the current directly d'homme à homme, with one sovereign individual awakening another.

Under classical Martinist jurisprudence (upheld across Papusian, Bricaud, and Ambelain traditions), every duly initiated 3° Supérieur Inconnu (S∴I∴) represents a complete, self-contained spiritual node capable of transmitting the foundational current person-to-person up to the 2° (Initié), while a holder of the 4° warrant (Supérieur Inconnu Initiateur, S∴I∴I∴) holds formal administrative authority to confer the 3°, charter Lodges or Heptads, and issue 4° warrants.

Across Europe and North America, independent Initiateurs Libres have preserved these lineages outside institutional politics, ensuring that the current remains accessible to serious practitioners regardless of sectarian affiliation.
